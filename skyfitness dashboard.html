Project Overview

The Sky Fitness Dashboard is an end-to-end business intelligence solution built in Microsoft Power BI for a gym and fitness management business. The project transformed a manually operated reporting process into a fully automated, interactive dashboard delivering real-time visibility across all key areas of the business.

Goal: Replace manual gym reporting with a centralized, interactive dashboard that enables faster, data-driven decisions across operations, finance, membership management, and member health tracking.

Dashboard Modules

  • 📈 Business performance & KPI monitoring
  • 📅 Monthly financial trend analysis
  • 🏅 Membership status tracking & retention management
  • 🧮 Interactive fitness & health calculator
  • 👤 Individual member profile views

Business Requirements

1. Overall Business Performance

The gym previously relied on manual summaries to monitor key operational metrics. The dashboard automates and visualizes these in a single centralized view:

  • Total number of clients/members and trainers
  • Total revenue generated
  • Total expenses incurred
  • Total profit earned

2. Monthly Performance Tracking

Management required monthly trend analysis for financial and operational metrics:

  • Monthly member acquisition trends
  • Monthly revenue, expenses, and profit
  • Revenue vs. expenses comparison visuals
  • Profit trend analysis over time

3. Membership Status Tracking

To improve customer retention, the dashboard tracks active and expired memberships across all tiers — Platinum, Gold, and Silver — and provides quick visibility into:

  • Active vs. expired member counts per tier
  • Expiring and cancelled memberships
  • Membership progress indicators

4. Interactive Fitness & Health Calculator

An interactive fitness calculator was built directly into the dashboard. Users input their age, weight, height, gender, and activity level to receive dynamic, personalized calculations for:

  • BMI – Body Mass Index
  • BMR – Basal Metabolic Rate
  • TDEE – Total Daily Energy Expenditure
  • Calorie targets for weight maintenance and weight loss

5. Member Detailed Profile

Each member has a dedicated profile view providing a centralized record of:

  • Personal information and assigned trainer
  • Membership type, start date, and expiry
  • Fitness goals, current BMI, and membership status
  • Progress indicators

Data Preparation & Transformation

1. Data Quality Checks

Before importing into Power BI, the dataset was reviewed in Excel to identify and resolve missing values, incorrect data types, duplicate records, and inconsistent entries.

2. Dynamic Membership Status Column

The original status column was static and did not update over time. A new DAX calculated column was created to make membership status dynamic: 

Status =
IF(
    Users[MembershipEnd] < TODAY(),
    "Expired",
    "Active"
)

This ensures statuses automatically reflect the current date every time the report is refreshed.

3. Calendar Table

A dedicated calendar table was created to support time intelligence functions and monthly trend analysis:

Interactive Fitness Calculator

Power BI parameters were created to allow users to dynamically input their personal data. These feed into DAX measures that calculate four key fitness metrics in real time.

BMI — Body Mass Index

BMI = Weight (kg) / [Height (m)]²

BMR — Basal Metabolic Rate

BMR is calculated differently by gender (Mifflin–St Jeor equation):

Men:   (10 × weight kg) + (6.25 × height cm) − (5 × age) + 5
Women: (10 × weight kg) + (6.25 × height cm) − (5 × age) − 161

TDEE — Total Daily Energy Expenditure

TDEE = BMR × Activity Factor

Case Study: Ellen

Ellen is a 25-year-old woman, 1.5 m tall, weighing 70 kg, with a sedentary lifestyle. Here are her calculator outputs:

Monthly Performance

November was the strongest month of the year — 19 active members, $680K in revenue, and $624K in profit, making it the peak period across all three metrics.

March and April were the weakest months. March recorded only 5 members and April 7, with both generating $230K in revenue. April posted the lowest profit at $110K due to higher relative expenses.

The November-to-March variance is a significant operational signal. Management should investigate whether this reflects seasonality, marketing gaps, or structural issues in member retention and acquisition.

Across all tiers, expired memberships significantly outnumber active ones. This points to a substantial retention opportunity and suggests the gym would benefit from a structured renewal outreach programme.

Member Demographics

The 40–60 age group accounts for 53% of total gym membership — an atypical profile for a fitness facility, which usually skews younger.

This warrants further investigation: is the gym actively attracting older adults through targeted programming, or are there barriers — pricing, culture, or scheduling — discouraging younger members from joining or staying? Older male members currently hold the highest proportion of active memberships, making them the gym's most engaged demographic segment.
